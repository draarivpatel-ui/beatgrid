BeatGrid is a silent studio for Euclidean rhythms that runs entirely offline in the browser. It makes no network requests and plays no audio; the work happens as analysis on screen and as files you export.

Three voices open the session: Tresillo, Seven in twelve, and Four in nine. Their cycle lengths realign every seventy-two steps, exactly nine seconds at one hundred twenty beats per minute. Each step is one sixteenth note at four steps per beat.

Every pad is editable. Toggling a cell turns the voice into a manual pattern, and one button restores the Euclidean form. Arrow keys move across the grid, and a preset chip adds another voice, up to eight.

The pattern inspector reports cyclic onset gaps, onset density, step evenness, and the shortest repeating vector. The phase wheel plots every voice on a single angular axis, and a step ledger lists the first ninety-six steps in order.

Coincidence and Hamming matrices compare every pair of voices across the whole cycle, here thirty-two shared hits in seventy-two steps. The syncopation panel scores each onset against a four-four meter grid.

Exports cover four formats: a validated session file, a canonical pattern set, a MIDI format-zero file with a byte-level inspector, and LilyPond percussion source. Pasting a session back restores the workspace exactly; a rejected import changes nothing. Every download is generated locally, byte by byte.

Voice lengths run to sixty-four steps, with shared cycles capped at four thousand ninety-six. Two hundred thirty-nine tests keep the math honest. BeatGrid turns a rhythm idea into notation, numbers, and files, with nothing leaving the machine.
